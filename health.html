Sleep

Plan your night, backwards.

Sleep comes in cycles of about 90 minutes. Waking at the end of one feels easier than waking in the middle. Most adults need 7–9 hours.

Assumes about 15 minutes to fall asleep. Individual needs vary.

6 / 5 / 4 cycles · 9 h / 7.5 h / 6 h of sleep

01

Keep a schedule

Go to bed and wake at similar times, even on weekends. Your body clock loves regularity.

02

Dim the lights

Lower lights and put screens away 30–60 minutes before bed. Keep the room cool, dark and quiet.

03

Mind the caffeine

Caffeine can linger for 6+ hours. Try stopping by early afternoon, and avoid heavy meals late at night.

Hydration

How much water?

A common rule of thumb is 30–35 ml per kg of body weight a day, from all drinks and food. Heat, exercise, pregnancy and health conditions change it.

2.3litres / day

≈ 9 glasses of 250 ml

THE CHECK

Pale yellow is the goal

Urine colour is a simple guide: pale straw means you’re doing well; dark amber means drink more.

THE HABIT

Anchor it

A glass on waking, one with each meal and one with each workout covers most of the day.

FOODS COUNT

Eat your water

Cucumber, oranges, melon, soups and yoghurt all add fluid.

Nutrition

The plate method.

No counting, no banned foods. Build most meals like this and add a little healthy fat.

Half: colour

Vegetables and fruit in as many colours as you can. They supply fibre, vitamins and water, and help skin and digestion.

Quarter: protein

Fish, eggs, poultry, beans, lentils, tofu, yoghurt. Protein supports muscle, hair and skin repair; include some at every meal.

Quarter: whole grains

Brown rice, oats, whole-wheat roti or bread, quinoa. Slow, steady energy and fibre.

A little fat

Olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado. Healthy fats help you absorb some vitamins and keep you satisfied.

Go easy on

Sugary drinks, heavily processed snacks and very salty foods. Enjoy treats without guilt, in moderation.

Calm

Four in. Four hold. Four out.

Box breathing slows the heart rate and settles the mind in a minute or two. Follow the circle: breathe in as it grows, hold, breathe out as it shrinks, hold.

Stress & mind

Small resets, big difference.

Step outside

Ten minutes of daylight and a short walk lowers tension and helps your sleep rhythm.

Write it down

Jot tomorrow’s to-do list or three things that went well. It clears the mind before bed.

Connect

A short call or chat with someone you trust is one of the best stress relievers there is.

Single-task

Do one thing at a time, in 25-minute blocks, with a short break. Less switching, less strain.

Limit the scroll

Set a daily time cap for social media and keep the phone out of the bedroom.

Ask for help

Feeling low, anxious or overwhelmed for more than a couple of weeks is a good reason to talk to a doctor or counsellor.

“You can fully catch up on sleep at weekends.”

Tap to flip
Partly

A lie-in helps a little, but a steady schedule is far better for mood, skin and energy.

Fact?

“Poor sleep shows on your skin.”

Tap to flip
Fact

Short sleep is linked to dullness, puffiness and more visible fine lines, and it slows skin recovery.

When to see a professional

Persistent fatigue, unexplained weight change, long-lasting low mood, ongoing sleep trouble, or any symptom that worries you deserves a conversation with a doctor. Soma gives general information, not medical advice.
